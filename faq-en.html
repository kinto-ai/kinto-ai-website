Frequently Asked Questions

What business owners usually ask before the first call.

What processes can be automated in my company?

The ones someone on your team does by hand every day or every week, following the same steps. Real examples from delivered projects:

· Bookings that arrive by email and someone copies one by one into a system.
· Accounting reconciliations reviewed line by line against the tax authority.
· Cost reports that take days to build every time they are requested.
· Payrolls and commissions calculated by hand every pay period.

The simplest test: if you can explain the process step by step, it can almost certainly be automated.

Do I need to know about technology to work with KINTO AI?

No. You explain your process in your own words, the way you would explain it to a new employee, and KINTO AI takes care of the technical side. The tools are delivered ready for your team to use without technical knowledge, with training included.

How much does it cost?

It depends on the process and the savings it generates. In the discovery call we estimate those savings and quote based on that, before anything is built and with no commitment.

How long does a project take and what do I need?

A typical project is delivered in weeks, not months. On your side, two things are needed: access to the files or systems where the process lives, and someone on your team who knows it well to validate that the result is correct. The rest is on KINTO AI.

Am I locked into KINTO AI, or does the system stay with my company?

The system stays in your company's own accounts, documented: it does not depend on you continuing to work with KINTO AI. Each project also includes a post-delivery support period, whose terms are defined in each project's proposal, plus an optional monthly maintenance plan if you need it.

How does a project start?

By writing to KINTO AI on WhatsApp, where you get an immediate reply. There we understand the process that is eating your time and, if there is a clear opportunity, the next step is estimating together how much time and money your company would save, and a formal proposal is presented on that number. Nothing gets built until the proposal is accepted; nothing moves forward without your written approval.
